INC-2026-09-11 — Water can shown as ₹92,213.10 spend instead of ₹5.

What broke: Amounts.first() matched `(?:Rs\.?|INR)\s*([0-9,]+\.[0-9]{1,2})`.
The decimal point was mandatory, so "Rs.5 debited ... Avl Bal: Rs.92,213.10"
skipped ₹5 and find() landed on ₹92,213.10 — the balance. 38 messages in
corpus-a were misparsed this way (transaction amount plus a balance); a
further 6 were dropped as non-transactions because no decimal-bearing figure
appeared at all.

How found: The regression test testExtractsTransactionAmountAndIgnoresBalance
reproduces the incident SMS with `expected: <5.00> but was: <92213.10>`.
Tracing Amounts.AMOUNT showed the pattern never matched the no-decimal amount.

Who: Any account whose bank writes whole-rupee transaction amounts. In
corpus-a that is 4821 and 9075; card account 3310 is unaffected because card
messages always write two decimals.

Fix: AMOUNT now allows an optional decimal (`(?:\.[0-9]{1,2})?`), and
first() truncates the body at the first balance marker (Avl Bal / Available
Balance / BalAvl / Avl Limit) before scanning. The regression test is in the
suite.

Recurrence: The pattern and the balance-marker split are both covered by the
test. A message whose first rupee figure is a balance can no longer be
returned by first().
